HARDER

A movement for harder digital futures.

HARDER is a network on a mission to refuse the idea that technology must be made comfortable before it is made ours. We are an unfinished platform built on an anarchist consciousness. We hold that harder values — refusal, autonomy, confrontation, friction, solidarity and teeth — are the only honest way to take our tools back from the people who own them.

Convergence Convergence2026

Harder Digital Futures 2026

The gathering returns — louder, and with the exits clearly marked.

What if the future of technology was not about moving slower and being kinder, but about asking who owns the machine, and taking it back? For three days we bring together hackers, organisers, repairers, artists, coders and refuseniks to build the digital futures we are not being offered. Through teardown workshops, repair clinics, self-hosting labs, digital self-defence training, reading groups and loud public argument, Convergence asks what technology looks like when it answers to us and to no one above us. Tickets are pay-what-you-can. No logos. No sponsors with something to launder.

Lab Repair

Own the machine, or it owns you

Every sealed device is an argument: that you are a user, not an owner — a tenant in hardware you paid for. We disagree with a soldering iron. We flash, we self-host, we keep the old machines running out of principle and spite. A computer you can open is a computer you can trust. Reclaim the right to understand what you hold.

School Essay

Hardware isn't scary. Ownership is forbidden.

They didn't make the machine mysterious. They made it a crime to understand — glue where there were screws, locked bootloaders, warranty-void stickers, firmware you may use but never read. That fog in your head when you open the case is not incompetence. It was designed, and it was sold to you. The computer is not beyond you. It was put beyond you on purpose.

Assembly Argument

They learned to say care

The industry absorbed its own critique and sold it back. It appointed heads of ethics, softened the fonts, rounded the corners, published manifestos about wellbeing and slowness — and the surveillance, the extraction, the enclosure grew right through it. Softer language, same machine. Kindness without power is a brand. Care without confrontation is customer service.

Assembly Origin

Why HARDER

We kept being told the problem with technology was its tone — too cold, too male, too hard. We agreed. Then we watched the same companies adopt the warmer tone and change nothing underneath it. You can make the cage more comfortable, or you can pick the lock. We decided to learn to pick locks. Comfort is not the same as freedom.
