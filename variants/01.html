liner notes what's this?

A tiny jungle machine: a chopped-up break, a reese bassline and some soft pads, all synthesised live in your browser with the Web Audio API. Nothing sampled, nothing downloaded, and it never plays until you press play. Switch patterns, chop it up, pull it up and go again \(^o^)/

credit the amen, credited

Every jungle machine owes the amen break: a few seconds of Gregory Coleman's drumming on The Winstons' "Amen, Brother" (1969). It's one of the most sampled recordings ever, and the band were never paid for it. This one plays a synthesised copy of the pattern rather than a sample, which is either very respectful or very funny. Who gets credited and paid when culture gets copied around the internet is an internet equalities question too.

respect where it comes from

Jungle grew up in early-90s London, out of Black British sound system culture: reggae and dancehall sounds, pirate radio, the hardcore rave scene. This page borrows the look (flyers, tape packs, tags, idents) with a lot of love, but it's a homage, not a costume. The real thing belongs to the people who made it, so go and listen to them, ideally loud.
