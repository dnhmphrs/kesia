respect due

jungle came out of black british sound system culture in london in the early 90s: reggae and dub sound systems, MCs, hip hop breaks and rave, cut to dubplate, passed round on tape packs and broadcast off tower block roofs on pirate radio. it was built by people the official channels mostly didn't make room for, so they made their own network. (an internet equalities person can't help noticing that.)

this page borrows the look. the culture belongs to the people who made it, so go and listen to them, not my laptop ✧

no samples were harmed

the amen break is a few seconds of drumming by gregory c. coleman on the winstons' "amen, brother" (1969). it's been sampled on thousands of records and the band never got royalties for it. so this machine doesn't sample it: every hit is synthesised in your browser in an amen-ish pattern, then sped up and chopped the way the old samplers did. who gets credited and paid when culture gets copied is an internet equalities question too.
