liner notes (credit where it's due)

jungle came up in london in the early 90s, out of Black British sound system culture: reggae and dub, ragga, hip hop and breakbeat hardcore, carried by pirate radio, dubplates and tape packs. this page borrows its look, so it should say where the look comes from.

the amen break is four bars of drums played by Gregory C. Coleman on The Winstons' “Amen, Brother” (1969). it's one of the most sampled recordings ever made, and neither he nor the band were paid royalties for it.

so the radio up there doesn't sample it. every hit is synthesised in your browser: an impression, not the real thing.

pirate radio was people building their own infrastructure when the official airwaves wouldn't play their music. who gets to broadcast, who gets credited, who gets paid — that's an internet equalities question too.
