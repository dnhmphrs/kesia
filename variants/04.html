hello, i'm keskes✦✧♡

that's kesia inkersole on paper. i'm a freelance creative strategist and internet equalities researcher in london.

my practice focuses on understanding and critiquing the impacts of emerging technologies on people, culture, and the planet. i'm particularly interested in working collectively to rethink the narratives and behaviours surrounding technologies, prioritising collaborative research as a process for testing, imagining and prototyping more equitable, alternative futures.

liner notes ✎

jungle is a london sound. it came out of black british sound system culture (reggae and dancehall sounds, MCs, dubplates, pirate radio) crashing into breakbeat hardcore in the early 90s. i'm borrowing its clothes for my homepage, so the least i can do is the credits.

the amen break is four bars of gregory c. coleman drumming on the winstons' "amen, brother" (1969). it's one of the most sampled recordings ever made, and the band never saw royalties for it. who makes the culture, who gets credited, who gets paid: an internet equalities question from before the internet.

radio kes doesn't sample anything. every kick, snare and reese is synthesised in your browser, on your device, right now. nothing downloads and nothing tracks you. the rhythm is still his though, so: big up gregory c. coleman.
