kes fm (the jungle machine)

it's a tiny jungle machine, and it stays quiet until you press play. ■ stop (or esc) cuts it straight off. it starts at a sensible volume, but maybe check yours first.

everything you hear is synthesised in your browser, live. there are no samples: a little drum break gets built from scratch at 136bpm, then sped up to 170, chopped and rewound, which is roughly how jungle producers flipped the amen.

  • ✂ chop reshuffles the break (changes land on the next bar)
  • ⟲ pull up rewinds it and brings it back from the top
  • tuning moves it between 160 and 175bpm
  • ✧ sparkles is the cute bit, ⚠ siren is a dub siren

credit where it's due

jungle came out of london in the early 90s, out of Black British sound system culture: reggae and dub sound systems, MCs, breakbeats and hardcore rave, carried on pirate radio, dubplates and tape packs. this page borrows the look, so it should say where the look comes from.

the amen break is a few seconds of Gregory C. Coleman's drumming on The Winstons' "Amen, Brother" (1969). it's one of the most sampled recordings ever, and the band never saw royalties for it. the machine here doesn't touch the recording, but the pattern is still his. who gets credited (and paid) when culture gets copied around the internet is an internet equalities question too.
