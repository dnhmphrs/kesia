kes fm 170.0 ((•)) the jungle machine ♡

a tiny pirate station that lives in this page. press play and your browser builds a jungle beat from scratch: an amen-style break at 170bpm, a reese bass, and a sub underneath it. pull up rewinds it, chop cuts the break up, and horn is a horn.

(｡･ω･｡) standing by. nothing plays till you press play.

sleeve notes credits where they're due ✿

  • jungle came out of Black British sound system culture in London in the early 90s: reggae, dub and dancehall bass weight, MCs, hip hop breaks and hardcore rave, sped up and passed around on pirate radio and tape packs. this page borrows the vocabulary. it doesn't own any of it.
  • the amen break is a few seconds of drums from "Amen, Brother" by The Winstons (1969), played by G.C. Coleman. it's one of the most sampled recordings ever, and neither Coleman nor the band were paid for its second life. the machine up there plays a synthesised version of his pattern, so: thank you, G.C.
  • pirate radio, run off tower block roofs with a text-in number read out between tunes, is where a lot of this music got heard first.
  • graffiti writers made the tags, throw-ups, drips and wildstyle arrows this page is doing impressions of. the throw-up up top is css, on my own wall.
  • kaomoji come from japanese text culture (ﾉ◕ヮ◕)ﾉ*:･ﾟ✧
