respect due (the liner notes)

this page borrows its loudness from jungle, rave and graffiti, so it should say where they come from. jungle came up in london in the early 90s out of Black British sound system culture — reggae and dancehall sound systems, hardcore raves, and pirate stations broadcasting off tower blocks before legal radio caught up. the graf look belongs to writers who did it for real, on walls and trains.

the amen break was played by Gregory C. Coleman of The Winstons, on “Amen, Brother” (1969). it's one of the most sampled recordings ever, and neither he nor the band's leader, Richard L. Spencer, were paid royalties for it. who gets credited and paid when culture gets copied and remixed online is an internet equalities question too.

so kes fm doesn't sample anything: every sound is synthesised in your browser, and the break is a re-drumming. this page won't put on a voice or call anything “urban”. lorem ipsum dolor sit amet, further reading to come.
