    liner notes: how it works

    the drums are synthesised into one two-bar break, an impression of the amen rather than a sample of it, then cut into 32 slices. the numbers in the little window are the slices. each pattern plays them back in a different order, which is roughly how jungle got made: take a break, chop it up on a sampler or a tracker, rearrange it, speed it up. move the tempo and the break gets pitched up or down with it, like it would on a sampler.

    the reese is two detuned saw waves rubbing against each other, named after kevin saunderson's "just want another chance", which he released as reese. the sub is a sine wave with a bit of grit so it still comes through laptop speakers. escape stops everything.

    respect due

    the amen break is four bars of gregory c. coleman drumming on the winstons' "amen, brother" (1969). it's one of the most sampled recordings ever made. coleman never got paid for it, and he died homeless in 2006. who gets credited and paid when culture gets copied and passed around is an internet equalities question as much as a music one.

    jungle came out of london in the early 90s, out of black british sound system culture (reggae, dub and dancehall), breakbeat hardcore raves and pirate radio. graf runs alongside it: goldie was a graffiti writer before he made "timeless". this page borrows the look and some of the language. the culture belongs to the people who built it, so go and listen to them instead of my little machine: kool fm, the ragga twins, congo natty (rebel mc), shy fx, dj rap, kemistry & storm.
